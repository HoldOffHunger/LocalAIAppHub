# AI Workers

Small local models doing narrow, well-defined jobs one item at a time, overnight, on Ben's 1070 Ti. Each job finds problems and reports them. It never edits a live site. A person, with Claude, confirms the findings and ships the fixes.

## earthfluent/: translation triage

This job rechecks EarthFluent's Google-translated vocabulary (`EntryTranslation`), one row at a time, judged against the row's **lesson**. The lesson is the sense: in Animals, Turkey is the bird, and in Countries it's the country. The method and record format belong to `E:\GGCMS\Development\TranslationReview\README.md`, and this worker only feeds the front of that pipeline.

- `dump_reader.py`: reads a language's rows, their English words and their lessons from a mysqldump (read-only). The default dump is `E:\Code\DB-Backups\2026-09-06\earthfluent.sql.gz`.
- `dictionary_index.py`: turns a kaikki.org Wiktionary dump into a SQLite lookup (`data/dictionary-<lang>.sqlite`). Italian is at `E:\ModelWorkshop\dictionaries\italian\`.
- `review_queue.py`: the pile and every verdict, in `data/earthfluent-review.sqlite`. A run resumes where the last one stopped.
- `word_check.py`: one question per row to a local model, at temperature 0, as JSON: ok or suspect, plus kind, proposed and reason. The dictionary glosses go in as evidence.
- `triage_file.py`: writes suspects to `earthfluent/triage/<lang>.triage.txt` in TranslationReview's format, with `status: suspect` and `checked by: <model>, unconfirmed`. **A suspect is not a proposal.** Confirm it against a dictionary of record (Treccani for Italian) before it moves into `TranslationReview/<lang>.txt`.
- `run_check.py --lang it --limit 500 --lesson "Nouns - Animals%" --until 02:45`. Stop before 03:00, because Autonoma's free time uses the GPU from 3 to 6 AM.

## Foreman

`start_foreman.bat` opens the Foreman page at http://127.0.0.1:8720. Like the Warden, it's local-only and opened through a login link (`data/foreman_login.txt`).

- **Tasks:** the page is a card per task, showing its percentage and done/total. A task is a **kind** of work (a folder with a `worker.json`) aimed at a **target** (a site or a language), with a name, notes and an "in the night shift" switch. Tasks live in `data/foreman.sqlite`, and each task's pile and results in `data/tasks/task-<id>.*`. Deleting a card keeps those files.
- **worker.json** holds title, description, `targets`, command, `until_flag`, `stats` (prints one JSON line with `done` and `total`, plus any extras the card should show) and `results`. Placeholders: `{python}` (this venv), `{target}`, `{queue}` and `{results}` (the task's files).
- **Night shift:** a schedule with days and a start and end time. While the window is open, it works the active, unfinished tasks top to bottom, each once per window. Each gets its share of what's left of the window (at least 15 minutes), not all of it: until 18 Oct 2026 the first card took the whole window, and RevoltLib's 14,000 quotes meant YallHearingThis below it never had a turn. It waits while any `autonoma-*` model is loaded in Ollama, so the two never share the 8 GB card. It also waits while one of the App Hub's Chron Jobs is due or running (the 3 AM memory backup, at the very moment the window opens): no new turn starts until the job is done, and a turn already going finishes. Without Chron Jobs running, it carries on as before.
- **Until Ben wakes:** the night shift's second mode, "Keep going until I'm back at the PC". From the start time it waits until nobody has touched the keyboard or mouse for 15 minutes (Windows' own idle count, in `foreman/presence.py`), then works round after round in turns of at most an hour, since nobody knows how long the night will be. The first touch after that ends the night: the shift's own turn stops, and nothing more starts until the next night's start time. A task run by hand, or free time given by hand, is never stopped by it.
- **Rest** (the first button in the header): after a task's turn, the night shift and free time wait this many minutes before starting the next (`rest_minutes` in `data/foreman.json`, 5 since 4 Nov 2026, 0 for none). A turn that came straight back doesn't rest, and free time given by hand starts at once.
- **Run** a task from its card: "Give it an hour", N minutes, or to the end. Stop is in the header. One task runs at a time; the log is in `data/logs/`.
- **Give the workers [time]** in the header is Autonoma's "free time": the night shift's rotation, starting now, for 30 minutes to 8 hours. Stop ends it.
- **Targets are sites.** `"targets": "sites"` in a worker.json means every site with a dump in `E:\Code\DB-Backups\<date>\` (all GGCMS sites share one schema). Site names and one-line descriptions for prompts are in `shared/sites.py`.
- **Nothing reaches prod.** The plan, in Ben's words, is to push through GGCMS's own **modify.php**, because it americanises the fields and prepares dates (BC years are stored in the 3000–10000 AD range). So writing jobs save a `.jsonl` beside their results, with one record per line in modify.php's field names (`Quote`, `quote_Source`, `quote_Language`…). A push script that feeds those to modify.php, for a batch Ben has reviewed, is still to build.
- Code is in `foreman/`, with tests in `foreman/tests` (`.venv\Scripts\python -m pytest foreman/tests`).

## ggcms_spellcheck/: spellcheck text body

Targets revoltlib and revoltsource, read from the newest `E:\Code\DB-Backups\<date>\<site>.sql.gz` (`shared/dump_tables.py`). The first run does the mechanical pass (`fill_queue.py`); later runs only judge.

- A word goes to the models if it's in GGCMS's own misspelling lists (`EnglishMisspellings`, `IntensiveEnglishMisspellings`, read by `shared/ggcms_words.py`), or if it's lowercase, unknown to pyspellchecker, one edit from a real word, and used in fewer than 3 texts. Words from the American/British lists and from Ben's Tag rows count as right.
- Capitalised words are skipped: they're mostly names, and need a different job.
- A text ticked **Spell Check Immune** on modify.php (`TextBody.SpellCheckImmune`, GGCMS, 3 Nov 2026) is deliberate wordplay and isn't read at all, here or by the harvester. Entry 7090 (Book 3, Chapter 3: 4,255 coinages) is the first. A dump from before the column has no such field, and every text is read.
- A word split in two ("inhe ritance", "Com mune") is one candidate: two neighbours whose join is a dictionary word, when at least one half isn't. The whole span is the word, so a fix replaces both halves, and splits never go to the new-misspellings list.
- Old English passes mechanically: thou/hath/shew-style words, and `-est`/`-eth`/`'d`/`-ick`/final-`e` forms on a real stem ("blanchest", "pil'd", "musick", "olde"; "hohest" still counts as a slip). Plurals of real words ("abolitions") pass too. A model's "typo" whose fix is the word itself is recorded as fine.
- `refresh_queue.py --queue <pile>` brings a pile filled by an older reader up to date: unjudged texts are read again, judged verdicts are tidied by the rules above. Run on task 2 on 24 Oct 2026; the pile before it is `task-2.before-refresh.sqlite`.
- `typo_check.py` asks one question per word, with its sentence: typo or fine. mistral:7b screens, and gemma4:12b rechecks the typos. "When unsure, fine."
- Results are written to `data/tasks/task-<id>.results.txt`, grouped by entry, all unconfirmed. Typos that aren't in GGCMS's lists yet go to `…results.new-misspellings.txt`, as candidates to add to those lists.
- Ben americanises every field on modify.php, so British spellings aren't typos here. Finding them is its own mechanical task (still to build).

## ggcms_misspellings/: harvest misspellings for GGCMS's lists

The spellcheck fixes a text; this one teaches the lists. It reads every published English text, gathers each unknown lowercase word across texts, and asks which are slips worth adding to `EnglishMisspellings`. **Every pair it writes is unconfirmed: Claude confirms them before they go into the lists.**

- `harvest.py` is the mechanical pass. A word goes forward when it's in 2 or more texts (a slip in one text is that text's own fix, which the spellcheck's new-misspellings file already has), and a real word is 1 or 2 edits away. Words GGCMS already knows are left out, and so are splits.
- What it drops before any model sees it: halves of hyphenated or line-broken words ("socio" of socio-political, "zation" of organi- zation); short words always followed by a full stop in all 3 sentences (etc., vol., eds.); and words of 6 or more letters that one of the 13 Wiktionaries has, which are quotations. A shorter Wiktionary word is kept, because "teh" is Indonesian, and its languages go to the judge as a hint.
- `wiktionary_words.py` indexes the kaikki.org dictionaries in `E:\ModelWorkshop\dictionaries` into SQLite once, and indexes them again when a file's size changes. On 26 Oct 2026, 11 of the 13 downloads were found cut short and were fetched again.
- `run_harvest.py`: gemma4:12b judges each word in up to two of its sentences. It counts as a typo only if both say typo with the same fix. Results go to `data/tasks/task-<id>.results.txt`, most texts first, with sentences.
- RevoltLib, 26 Oct 2026: 62,453 unknown words, 46,580 in one text only, 9,753 candidates before the halves and abbreviations drop (about 975 fewer after). The first smoke run's two "typos" were both wrong (socio, and versa of vice versa); the drop now catches the first. Real slips (militaty, migt, litde, necassary) sit in the long tail of 2 and 3 texts, not at the head. Set phrases (ad hoc, ipso facto) still reach the judge, which mostly calls them fine.

## ggcms_doubled_letters/: doubled-letter slips for GGCMS's lists

Ben's idea, 3 Nov 2026: a doubled letter typed once or three times ("wordles", "wordlesss") is a common slip, so make both for every common word and add them to GGCMS's misspelling lists. The target is the letter pair (ss, ll, tt…). "ss" is the trial (task 23); the other pairs wait on Ben reading its results. **Every pair is unconfirmed**: Claude confirms them before they go into `Language/Words/`.

- `doubled.py` is the mechanical pass. The source words are pyspellchecker's English list (modern, inflected, with counts), most used first. Its floor of about 50 is the rarity cut, which leaves out Webster's diskindness and docoglossa. A slip is thrown out if it's a real word in pyspellchecker, GGCMS's American/British lists or the correct side of its misspelling lists ("dessert" → "desert"), or a real word plus an ending ("princess" → "princes", "timeliness" → "timelines"; the stem needs 4 or more letters, because Webster has "mis" but "mised" isn't mis + ed). Slips already in the lists are skipped.
- Webster's 1913 (the dictionaries site, `E:\Code\DB-Backups\2026-09-06\alldictionaries.sql.gz`) counts as real for the ending rule. A slip only Webster knows ("mispend", "disenter") is an old spelling a modern writer would mean as a slip, so it goes to the judge instead of being thrown out.
- `letter_judge.py`: gemma4:12b, one call per word. It decides whether the word is English worth guarding (not a name, place, people or foreign word: "agassiz", "assamese") and whether each open dropped slip is a real word the dictionaries missed. "When unsure, real." A tripled letter is never an English word, so it never asks about those, and a Webster word with nothing else to ask needs no call.
- Trial, 3 Nov 2026: the 30 most common "ss" words had every dropped slip already listed or real, so the work is in the long tail. On 30 words spread down the list it caught both names and kept calmnes, distresing, emisions, buttreses and brasware. It was wrong once: it read "randiness" as "readiness" and called it not English. About 20 s a call and 5,217 calls for "ss", so several nights; results run most used first, so it can stop anywhere.
- GGCMS's main list has `'mispell'=>['misspell']` the wrong way round, so "misspell" counts as the slip and "mispell" as right. 423 of its 48,357 slips are words pyspellchecker counts 100+ times (want ← went, were ← where, weep ← wheep, mug ← mugg); reported to Ben 3 Nov 2026.
- **Tested on Wikipedia** (task 24): the Wikipedia typo hunter takes a `doubled-<pair>` target, which hunts this worker's kept slips instead of GGCMS's lists (`wikipedia_typos/doubled_pairs.py`). It adds new slips every run as this worker judges more, most used first, unshuffled. The point is `task-<id>.results.pairs.txt`: per slip, how often Wikipedia has it and how often the judge called it a slip there. **Doubtful** pairs (called right more often than wrong) are real words or names the dictionaries missed, and come out before anything goes into the lists. Any typos it finds go to Social's Wikipedia typos card, which reads `doubled-%` targets as well as en.wikipedia (Social needs a restart to see that).
- First 8 slips, 3 Nov 2026: tripled slips are rare on Wikipedia (0 to 18 hits each) and nearly all in links, file names and references. The one find, SubSpace's "ASSS", was a server's acronym, so the hunter now skips any word written in capitals, in every hunt (it also cleared Kenyon College's CSAD from the main one). The hunter also skips a word in the article's own title (Nicotini), and drops a find whose fix is another word rather than a spelling of it (florescence → flourishing, kaber → charlock).
- **English Wiktionary** (3 Nov 2026): `shared/english_wiktionary.py` reads kaikki.org's raw dump (`E:\ModelWorkshop\dictionaries\english\raw-wiktextract-data.jsonl`, 26 GB, January 2026) once into `data/english-wiktionary.sqlite`: 1,056,345 English words and inflected forms, with Wiktionary's 5,190 misspelling entries kept in their own table, so "recieve" never counts as real. `doubled.py` counts its words as real for every letter filled from now on ("ss" was filled before it). 4,433 of its misspellings went into GGCMS's main list the same day, leaving out the ones already listed, those Wiktionary tags deliberate, humorous, Internet or childish (skool, krazy, gaem), British spellings, and four whose corrections don't start with a letter ('twas, étale), since `_0` is code, not a list. `shared/list_merge.py` does the adding: into a word's block or a new block in sorted place, each new line with its neighbour's line ending (the files mix LF and CRLF).
- First full round, 3 Nov 2026 (task 23 at 631 of 6,119 words): all 815 kept slips searched, 149 typos found on Wikipedia across 97 pairs, and a sample of 12 were all real (asssessed, posssibly, concesions, asassination, actreses). Only 10 pairs were doubtful, and their "right" readings were names, titles and other languages (Los Angeles, the band Endlesss, Latvian "profesors"). Of those, only "los" and "ches" are bad pairs, both Webster-only short words. Round 2 reads each slip's next ten hits, so "los" (472,666 hits) would have run for weeks: a test now stops a slip after 30 hits (`TEST_DEPTH`). The pair report is now written after every slip, because a run stopped from the Foreman never reaches its end.

## ggcms_quote/: fill quote

For every published text with no Quote, `quote_candidates.py` lifts up to 20 whole sentences that could stand alone, spread through the text. It skips footnotes, editors' brackets, URLs, numbers and words still split by line-break hyphens. mistral:7b shortlists three (numbered from 1), and gemma4:12b picks one, so **every quote is word for word from the text**. Source follows Ben's style, `"Work," by Author`: the work is the entry under the site's section, and the author comes from an Association Role/Author row. A page with no author on record gets an empty Source. Shared readers: `shared/site_texts.py` (texts with their work, author and existing child records) and `shared/task_queue.py` (a generic resumable pile).

RevoltLib, 18 Oct 2026: 14,355 texts need a quote, and 608 are too short to have one.

## yallhearingthis/: fill shows and artists

Fills YallHearingThis.com **on int** (Fumiko's WSL copy, `localhost:8000/?domain=yallhearingthis.com`) from youtube.com/@yallhearingthis: a show under `/shows/` for every video, short and stream (1,769 on 18 Oct 2026), and an artist under `/artists/` for every act a show reacts to, linked show → artist by a Role/Artist association, as RevoltLib links a writing to its author. This job writes to int, never to production. A person looks it over before anything is pushed.

- `channel.py`: yt-dlp, no API key. Metadata and thumbnail first, then one English caption track: her own if there is one, otherwise the automatic `en-orig`. YouTube's automatic `en` is a machine translation and the request it throttles hardest. A video YouTube files under another language (often the MV's, Korean) has only that translation, the hosts' English heard as Korean and turned back, so it gets no transcript and the report says NO ENGLISH CAPTIONS; local Whisper is the way to fill those later. A 429 raises `RateLimited`; the run waits ten minutes, tries once more, then stops, and the rest stays in the pile.
- `artists.py`: gemma4:12b names the artists from the title, tags and description, and the code throws out any name those don't contain. The facts come from Wikipedia and Wikidata, never from the model: the intro as the biography (cited, as RevoltLib's are), its first sentence as the description, birth or formation date, the official site, and a 1280-pixel picture. `data/yallhearingthis/artists.json` is the book of spellings and int entry ids. Wikimedia refuses a bot with no contact, so the User-Agent names the site's contact page.
- `quote_lines.py`: automatic captions have no sentences, so the model picks a numbered stretch of two or three caption lines, and may trim it. The trim is kept only if it's a run of the stretch's own words, so every quote is word for word what was said.
- `records.py`: modify.php's own field names. Every yallhearingthis column is utf8mb3, so emoji and symbols leave the plain fields, and the textbody keeps them as character references. Description is 512 characters, so it gets the video's own lines; the full YouTube description goes in the textbody, after a labelled block of every YouTube fact with no field of its own (format, published, duration, views, likes, comments, category, language, location, chapters…) and before the transcript. The title splits at its colon into title and subtitle, and smart title case is off, so NMIXX stays NMIXX.
- `int_site.py`: writes only through `modify_entry.php --fields-json`, via `wsl --exec` (no shell), as user 1. The site has two administrators, so `ADMIN` is refused as ambiguous. A show is known by its YouTube link, so a rerun never makes it twice.
- `run_yall.py --only <id>` does named videos first, `--dry` runs modify.php dry, and `--refresh` rebuilds shows (and, once a run, their artists) already on int and updates them in place.
- **Pushing to production** is a person's job, never the worker's. `prod_bundle.py <video ids>` packs shows already on int, with their artists' records and pictures, into `data/yallhearingthis/prod-bundle.tgz`. On the host, `php push_bundle.php /tmp/yht-bundle` dry-runs it and `--apply` does it: it makes the Shows and Artists sections if they're missing, then any missing artist, then each show, all through that host's `modify_entry.php`. A show names its artists by code, which become the host's own entry ids there. Anything already present is left alone, so running it twice is safe.
- Seeded 18 Oct 2026: NMIXX "Sonar", "DICE" and Tiny Desk, Billlie "Trampoline", and Billlie & NMIXX's holiday MV (two artists on one show).
- Int needed `php8.1-imagick` and `/srv/ggcms/yallhearingthis.com/www/image/` (owner www-data) before a picture would save. On int, an image URL only serves with `?domain=yallhearingthis.com` on it, so pages show broken pictures there. On production the host picks the site, and they serve.

## ship/: putting int's work live

Ben's rule (24 Oct 2026): run the job on int, warm on Fumiko, send the database and the warmed pages to prod. No review, no dry runs. Both must go: pages alone vanish at the first prod save, because modify.php flushes the whole site's cache on every save. These are the engine of the Foreman's Ship button, which is still to wire.

1. Before the job: `bash ship/checksum.sh <db>` on int **and** prod, and diff. Only logs, users and sessions may differ; keep int's file as `before.txt`.
2. Run the job on int (for quotes, `ggcms_quote/push_int.py`; it skips the paths two entries share, since saving through one wrote over its twin).
3. `bash ship/checksum.sh <db>` on int again; the tables that changed are the ones to ship, plus `ChildRecordStats AssociatedRecordStats`.
4. `bash ship/warm_site.sh <site>` (RevoltLib: 15,340 pages in 8 minutes), then `bash ship/pack.sh <site> <db> /mnt/d/<x>-ship before.txt "<tables>" [--images]`.
5. scp the folder to `/mnt/nyc01/` (the root disk is 80% full), then on prod `bash swap_tables.sh <folder> <db> <site>` and `bash ship_pages.sh <folder> <site>`. The swap refuses if prod changed since `before.txt`, backs up first, and leaves the old tables in `<db>_before_ship`.

The auto-mode classifier blocked Claude's prod DELETE on 24 Oct even with Ben's go-ahead, though uploads, the swap and the pages weren't blocked. If it blocks again, stage the files in `/tmp` and hand Ben the one-line command.

## ggcms_wikilinks/: Wikipedia dead and missing links

Finds English Wikipedia articles where a link to a text we host would help, and writes the edit for Ben to make by hand. **It never edits Wikipedia or the site.** Ben's rules (29 Oct 2026): every link is the permalink, `https://<site>.com/?id=<Assignment.id>` as `entry-share.php`'s `PermalinkURL()` builds it, using the entry's oldest Assignment; and the edits go up slowly, with his conflict of interest disclosed, so the domain never looks like link spam.

- **Our side** (`our_texts.py`): `TextBody.Source` is filled on 14,935 of RevoltLib's 15,102 texts (18 Oct 2026), but almost always with a site's *name* ("TheAnarchistLibrary.org", "Anarchy Archives"), not a page URL; only 35 hold a URL. Names become domains (`NAMED` holds the ones not spelled as a domain). A text with no Source counts a closing "Source:" line in its body, never a footnote's link. `…results.source-names.txt` lists the names it couldn't place, and `…results.sourceless.txt` the 140 texts with no source at all.
- **Wikipedia's side** (`wiki_dumps.py`, `wiki_index.py`): the `externallinks` (5 GB) and `page` (2.4 GB) tables from dumps.wikimedia.org, in `data/wikipedia/`, capped at **0.5 TB** (Ben's figure) and refused if E: would drop under 1 TB free. A download resumes after a stop. The link scan (about 1.1 billion rows, an hour or so) and the title scan save their place every two million lines. Since 2024 these dumps put one row on each line, unlike GGCMS's, so they have their own reader. `--refresh-dumps` fetches newer tables.
- **The pile**, in four kinds:
  - **link**: an article cites one of our Source URLs exactly.
  - **near**: an article links into a site we took texts from. Every link if the site is dead (judged from three of its own Wikipedia links, since a dead site's front page often still answers); for live sites, only links in our works' and authors' own articles.
  - **work** and **author**: an article whose title matches one of our works or authors and doesn't link us yet.
- **Judging**: a near link is matched by fetching the Internet Archive's copy and comparing it word for word with that site's texts (eight-word runs; `TextFinder` indexes one run in sixteen, so it doesn't compare with all 1,453 Anarchy Archives texts). 60% or more is "same text". gemma4:12b only decides whether a same-titled article is about our work or author; a title marked "(book)" whose opening names the author needs no model.
- **Politeness**: Wikipedia's API one request a second with `maxlag`; other sites one request per host every 3 s; the Archive every 4 s. The archived copy is looked up in the CDX index on web.archive.org (newest capture that answered 200): from about 3 Nov 2026 archive.org/wayback/available answered us 429 every time while CDX answered at once. When the Archive throttles or is offline, its items rest (5 minutes, doubling to an hour) and stay in the pile; once only its items are left, the turn ends rather than waiting.
- **Results**: `task-<id>.results.txt` in four parts, best first: dead links Wikipedia has marked, dead links nobody has marked, works' articles with no link to us, authors' articles with no link to us. Each has the citation as written, the ready-to-paste replacement (`|url=` swapped, `|via=RevoltLib` added, the dead-link mark dropped), an edit summary that discloses Ben runs the site, and for works and authors an External links line plus `{{Edit COI}}` talk-page text. A citation that already has an archive copy is marked low: ours would be an extra, not a fix.

## mia_wikilinks/: Wikipedia links to Marxists.org

The same search for the Marxists Internet Archive, which Ben volunteers for, over a local mirror of its English text pages (`E:\mia\site`, 88,597 of 88,600 `.htm` pages, finished 4 Nov 2026; the three missing have `</html>` and `>` in their names, which Windows refuses). `list_mia.py` and `fetch_mia.py` made it over SFTP, read-only. Wikipedia's links into marxists.org are borrowed from the RevoltLib task's index, so no link scan is needed. A task is one target, in one of two modes:

- **An author** (`marxists.org/archive/<name>`, `run_mia_links.py`): dead links into the author's folder, and articles about the author or a work with no link there. Works RevoltLib holds are left to the RevoltLib task.
- **A collection** (`marxists.org/history/…` or `marxists.org/subject/…`, `run_mia_subjects.py`, Ben's idea of 4 Nov 2026: the texts' own articles mostly link MIA already, so look for parties, newspapers, congresses, revolutions and movements). Every folder under the target with an index page and 3 or more pages is a collection. Its names are its index title, tidied ("Red Front: Index | Encyclopedia of Anti-Revisionism On-Line" is Red Front, "History of the Second International" is also Second International, "International Socialism (1st Series) 1958-1968" is also International Socialism), plus its folder's name when that's two words or more, or the only name there is. A one-word folder ("women", "texts") under a titled index page would match every general article. The code drops films, bands, teams and the like by their Wikipedia aside, disambiguation pages, and articles that already link the collection. gemma4:12b judges the rest from the collection's index text and the article's lead, and says no to a whole country or a person when the collection is narrower. Redirects that land on one article are judged once. Biggest collections first.

## archive_ocr/: OCR misreadings from the Internet Archive

Ben's idea, 2 Nov 2026: many of the Wikipedia typo hunter's finds were made by a scanner, not a typist ("suftiently", where the ffi ligature read as ft), so use them to find badly scanned texts on the Internet Archive, and mine those for the scanner's other habits. They are candidates for GGCMS's misspelling lists, and help catch Wikipedia prose pasted from old scans. **Nothing is added to any list automatically**, and no model is used: it never asks for the GPU.

- `ocr_shapes.py`: the classifier. A misspelling is OCR-shaped when look-alike swaps alone turn it into the right word: strong shapes (rn/m, cl/d, li/h, vv/w and iv/w, ii/u, the long s read as f, dropped ligatures) and weak single letters (b/h, c/e, c/o, n/u, l/i, l/t, c/t, v/y). Keyboard neighbours and vowel-for-vowel swaps are left out, because those are how people misspell ("tegether", "operater").
- `seeds.py`: reads the hunter's pile (`task-21.sqlite`) read-only. Its confirmed finds come first, then the rest of GGCMS's list it hunts. A seed must use only strong shapes (or have a judge's reason that says OCR or scan), have 4 or more letters, and not be a real word. Only one of the hunter's 492 finds by 2 Nov 2026 qualified ("identifed"), and the judge never said OCR, so the list does most of the seeding.
- `archive_client.py`: the Archive's full-text search works for guests only through the page service its own search page uses (`services/search/beta/page_production/`, `service_backend=fts`, `page_type=search_results`). The scrape API's `service=fts` refuses guests, and advancedsearch only searches metadata. One word a query, since two words or OR find nothing. Up to 100 hits come back, with OCR snippets, in 2 to 4 s. The client waits 5 s between requests. A 429 or 5xx ends the turn, and the pile carries on next time. Each OCR text (`_djvu.txt`, sized from `metadata/<id>/files`) is fetched only if it is 3 MB or less. Bigger ones are listed in the results for Ben to OK.
- `mine_queue.py`: seeds, the items each search found, and the words mined from each item. An item two or more different seeds found is a bad scan, and the worst are mined first (`--mine 60` in all, `--per-run 15`).
- `ocr_mining.py`: lowercase words of 4 or more letters, with line-break hyphens joined, that pyspellchecker doesn't know, and that one swap (two for words of 7 or more letters) turns into a word it counts at least 50 times. A candidate must recur in two or more separately scanned texts.
- Results go to `task-<id>.results.txt`, strong shapes first, then the most texts, with a line of OCR and the item's link. GGCMS's already-listed words are counted but left out. The same goes to `.results.json` for the App Hub's Social page, the "OCR misreadings" card.
